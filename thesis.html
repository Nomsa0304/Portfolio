FIG. 01 — MSC THESIS

New Generalized Families and Classes of Heavy-Tailed Distributions with Applications

MSc Statistics · Botswana International University of Science & Technology · March 2025
Statistical Theory R Maximum Likelihood Estimation Actuarial Risk Measures
Supervisors: Dr Fastel Chipepa & Professor Broderick Oluyede, Department of Mathematics & Statistical Sciences, BIUST

Abstract

This thesis develops three new generalized families of heavy-tailed probability distributions: the Heavy-Tailed-Type II Topp-Leone-G (HT-TIITL-G) distribution, the Heavy-Tailed-Type II Topp-Leone-G Power Series (HT-TIITL-GPS) distribution, and the Topp-Leone-Generalized Exponentiated Half Logistic-G (TL-GEHL-G) distribution. For each family, I derived core mathematical properties — moment generating functions, incomplete moments, linear representations, order statistics, and entropy — estimated parameters via maximum likelihood, and evaluated performance through Monte Carlo simulation. I also derived actuarial risk measures and benchmarked all three families against established models on real datasets using multiple goodness-of-fit tests. Across every dataset tested, the proposed distributions outperformed existing models at capturing heavy-tailed behavior.

Why heavy-tailed distributions

Standard distributions like Weibull, Pareto, and log-logistic are foundational in reliability engineering, survival analysis, and risk management, but each has real limits: Weibull's tail isn't heavy enough for extreme events, Pareto's shape is too rigid across different datasets, and log-logistic lacks the tail weight needed when modeling rare, extreme outcomes. This gap is exactly where heavy-tailed distributions matter — insurance claims, financial risk, equipment failure times, and cancer remission times all involve exactly the kind of extreme-value behavior these classical models struggle to capture. My thesis builds new distribution families specifically to close that gap.

Structure of the work

CH. 2

HT-TIITL-G Family

Introduces the base family, derives its quantile function, moments, order statistics, and Rényi entropy, and evaluates the HT-TIITL-Weibull special case on a reliability engineering dataset (cutting-machine failure times) and a bladder cancer remission dataset — outperforming the Weibull, TI-HTW, HLGW, and HT-BPTW models on every goodness-of-fit statistic tested.

CH. 3

HT-TIITL-GPS Class

Extends the family by compounding it with power series distributions (Poisson, geometric, binomial, logarithmic), producing the HT-TIITL-WP special case. Applied to analgesic relief-time data and bank customer waiting times, this model achieved the highest K-S p-value and lowest error statistics among six competing models.

CH. 4

TL-GEHL-G Family

Combines the Topp-Leone generator with the generalized exponentiated half-logistic distribution. The TL-GEHL-Weibull special case was tested on Kevlar-49/epoxy strand failure-time data and the same cancer remission dataset, again outperforming six alternative models including Marshall-Olkin Weibull-Logarithmic and Topp-Leone-Gompertz-Weibull.

Methods

  • Maximum likelihood estimation to fit every proposed distribution, with standard errors derived from the estimated variance-covariance matrix
  • Monte Carlo simulation (sample sizes from n=25 to n=1000, 3,000 replications per size) to confirm estimator bias and RMSE shrink as sample size grows
  • Actuarial risk measures — Value-at-Risk, Tail Value-at-Risk, Tail Variance, and Tail Variance Premium — derived analytically for each family and compared numerically against benchmark distributions
  • Goodness-of-fit testing — AIC, BIC, Cramér-von Mises, Anderson-Darling, and Kolmogorov-Smirnov statistics — across every real-data application, backed by Kaplan-Meier survival curves, PP plots, and TTT plots

Key findings

  • All three proposed families showed consistently heavier tails (higher VaR, TVaR, TV, TVP) than the classical Weibull and existing heavy-tailed benchmarks, confirming they capture extreme-value behavior these established models miss.
  • On every one of the six real datasets tested — spanning reliability engineering, oncology, pharmacology, banking, and materials science — a special case of one of the three new families was the best-fitting model, based on AIC/BIC and K-S p-values.
  • Simulation studies confirmed the MLE-based estimators are consistent: bias and RMSE shrink predictably as sample size increases from 25 to 1,000.
  • The hazard rate functions derived from these families can take on reverse-J, bathtub, and inverted-bathtub shapes — the range needed to model everything from early-life failures to wear-out failures in one framework.
