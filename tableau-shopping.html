Shopping Behavior Dashboard

Tableau · Consumer analytics · 3,900 customers over 2 years
Tableau Data Visualization Consumer Analytics Course Project

Overview

This dashboard analyzes a shopping behavior dataset covering roughly 3,900 customers, male and female, observed over two years across multiple seasons. The goal was to move from raw transaction records to a set of visualizations that a retail team could actually use to spot trends, compare product performance, and understand customer segments. Built with Beverly Sibanda for an Information Visualization course.

Objectives

  • Identify overall sales trends across the dataset
  • Analyze and compare performance across product categories, items, and attributes
  • Surface seasonal and time-based purchasing patterns
  • Identify customer segments and the relationships between them
  • Evaluate operational effectiveness (ratings, shipping, fulfillment signals)

Data

The dataset combined categorical fields (gender, customer ID, category, age, item purchased, size, color, season, shipping type) with quantitative fields (purchase amount and previous purchase amount). Each chart in the dashboard was built to answer one specific question from the objectives above, using a mark type and encoding chosen to fit that question rather than a one-size-fits-all chart style.

The dashboard

Findings

  • Category performance was uneven. The treemap made it immediately clear which items pulled the most customers, rather than just the most revenue — a distinction that matters for inventory decisions.
  • Purchase behavior varied meaningfully by gender across categories, visible in the bar chart comparison, which suggested different marketing angles could apply to each segment.
  • Geography wasn't uniform. The state-level map showed purchase volume concentrated in specific regions rather than spread evenly, useful for regional stocking or shipping decisions.
  • Season had a visible effect on purchase amount by category, supporting seasonal planning over a flat, year-round strategy.
  • Color and size preference clustered around a handful of dominant choices rather than spreading evenly, shown in the bubble chart.
